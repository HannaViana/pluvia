Dear Editor-in-Chief Fei Chen, 

I am submitting our manuscript entitled “Urban Flooding in a Tropical Coastal Environment: Spatiotemporal Patterns for Early Warning and Resilience” to Urban Climate on February 28, 2026. 

Urban flooding is a recurrent challenge in Rio de Janeiro, where intense rainfall interacts with complex terrain, coastal influences, and heterogeneous drainage conditions to produce localized yet highly disruptive impacts on mobility and public safety. In this study, we integrate high-temporal-resolution rainfall observations from the Alerta Rio rain-gauge network with georeferenced operational records of water accumulation and street flooding to characterize spatiotemporal patterns of impacts across the municipality. We segment rainfall into independent events, quantify key event properties (duration and maximum 1-hour intensity), classify impacts by operational severity, and establish a reproducible rainfall–impact pairing framework using Thiessen (Voronoi) station influence areas and temporal overlap. Our results document clear severity-dependent persistence, strong seasonality and diurnal clustering, and persistent spatial hotspots, while also demonstrating strong representativeness of the rain-gauge network for the occurrence dataset. These findings provide an evidence base to support the subsequent development of locally meaningful rainfall thresholds and targeted preparedness actions.

## **Highlights:** 

- Integrated 15-min rain-gauge observations with 4,868 georeferenced urban flood occurrence records to build an impact-based dataset for Rio de Janeiro. 

- Quantified severity and persistence of impacts: moderate ponding dominates (86.6%), while high-severity events last longer and show heavier duration tails. 

- Revealed strong seasonality and diurnal clustering, with warm-season late-afternoon/evening peaks aligned with highest mobility exposure. 

- Mapped recurrent spatial hotspots and severity-dependent footprints, supporting targeted preparedness and infrastructure prioritization.

The central premise is that identifying discrete rainfall events at each station enables consistent comparison `28 29` 150 across hydrometeorological episodes and facilitates evaluation of temporal coincidence with recorded impacts in the `30` 151 urban environment. The continuous rainfall time series at each station was segmented into discrete rainfall events to `31 32` 152 support intensity-duration analyses. A rainfall event was defined as a period with continuous rainfall records, separated `33` 153 from the subsequent event by a minimum dry interval of 6 hours. The definition of independent rainfall events and the `34 35` 154 choice of a minimum inter-event time are widely recognized as critical steps because they directly affect derived event `36` 155 properties such as duration, peak intensity, and event totals [21,22,23]. Sensitivity of event statistics to inter-event `37 38` 156 separation criteria has also been documented across climatic settings, reinforcing that the selected dry interval is an `39 40` 157 operational decision that should be explicitly stated and justified [24,25,26,27]. `41 42` 158 In this study, the 6-hour interval was used to reduce the merging of distinct episodes and to represent each `43 44` 159 event as an independent unit for subsequent pairing with occurrence records within the same temporal window [22,25]. `45` 160 For each rainfall event identified at each station, two parameters were computed. Duration (D) was defined as the total `46 47` 161 event length in hours, from the first to the last rainfall record. Maximum intensity (I) was defined as the highest rainfall `48` 162 rate observed during the event, computed as the maximum accumulated rainfall over a 1-hour window, corresponding `49 50` 163 to four consecutive 15-minute measurements, expressed in millimeters per hour. ## 170 **2.4 Processing and classification of urban flooding occurrences** 

`1 2` 171 Urban flooding occurrence records were obtained from COR Rio, which compiles georeferenced reports of `3 4` 172 street-level water accumulation and flooding impacts across different sectors of the municipality during the analyzed `5` 173 period. After selecting records related to urban flooding impacts, data cleaning was performed to remove duplicates `6 7` 174 and exclude occurrences with invalid geographic coordinates. This step is necessary to ensure spatial consistency `8` 175 when associating with station influence areas and to avoid redundant entries that artificially inflate occurrence `9 10` 176 frequencies. For each occurrence, event duration was calculated as the difference between the end time and the start `11` 177 time recorded in the database, providing a persistence metric of the impact. This variable serves as a complementary `12 13` 178 indicator of operational severity, since more persistent occurrences typically require longer response times and may `14 15` 179 imply longer mobility disruption. Type 1 corresponds to shallow water accumulation between 0 and 15.0 centimeters `24 25` 185 and represents an initial stage of accumulation with low operational impact. Type 2 corresponds to water ponding `26` 186 between 15.0 and 30.0 centimeters, in which light vehicle traffic remains possible but impaired, and pedestrians are `27 28` 187 typically required to enter the water to cross the street. This is the most frequent class in the database, representing `29` 188 86.6 percent of the selected occurrences. Type 3 corresponds to street flooding between 30.0 and 50.0 centimeters and `30 31` 189 is characterized by fully preventing light vehicle traffic, being considered a higher operational severity condition. ## **4. Conclusions** 

`1 2` 592 This study integrated high-temporal-resolution rainfall observations with georeferenced, operationally `3 4` 593 classified records of urban flooding occurrences to characterize when, where, and how long impacts persist in Rio de `5` 594 Janeiro, and to establish a reproducible rainfall-impact pairing framework to support subsequent threshold `6 7` 595 development. By combining impact records with station-based rainfall event definitions, the analysis moves beyond `8` 596 rainfall-only characterization and provides an impact-oriented evidence base directly aligned with municipal `9 10` 597 operations. The results demonstrate that impact occurrences are highly structured in time and space rather than `11` 598 randomly distributed across the city, reinforcing the value of impact-based diagnostics for operational monitoring, `12 13` 599 infrastructure prioritization, and urban resilience planning. In this sense, the study contributes a practical pathway for `14 15` 600 converting routinely collected operational information into actionable knowledge for early warning and preparedness. `16 17` 601 Moderate-severity road ponding impacts dominate the occurrence database, while high-severity street `18 19` 602 flooding events represent a smaller fraction but exhibit substantially greater persistence. Duration distributions show `20` 603 clear severity dependence: pooled low- and moderate-severity events resolve more quickly, while high-severity events `21 22` 604 exhibit longer typical durations and a heavier upper tail. This persistence gradient indicates that severity is not only a `23` 605 function of depth-related operational classification.
